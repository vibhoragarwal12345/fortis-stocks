The CEFA Engine · Full detail

From 3,300 Stocks to 15 Conviction Picks

Twice every weekday the entire liquid US market runs through one two-speed funnel: ~3,300 → 35 → a displayed board of 15–25. The math lets us look at every stock; the AI turns the survivors into research. Neither half works alone.

Step 0 — Fresh intelligence, gathered before the scan

About 90 minutes before the pre-market scan, a separate harvest refreshes the facts the analysis stands on. It runs apart from the scan, so a flaky source degrades freshness but never breaks the run.

The principle throughout: if the data isn’t there, the system records “no data” — it never invents one.

Stage 1 — The fast scan: pure math on the whole market

~3,300 stocks → all measured. No AI. ~3 minutes. Objective and identical for every stock. A name must trade at least $1M average daily volume to qualify (the universe rebuilds weekly). From one year of daily prices we compute:

  • Price action — today’s move and the overnight gap
  • Momentum — 5- and 20-day return
  • Relative volume — vs. the stock’s own 20-day average
  • RSI (14-day) — the Wilder momentum oscillator
  • 52-week position — distance from its 52-week high and low
  • Breakout / breakdown — closed above the prior 20-day high, or below the low

This is measuring, not judging — and robust to thin data: a recent IPO returns “not enough data” rather than a misleading number.

Stage 2 — The composite score: ranking the whole market into a top 35

~3,300 scored → top 35 kept. No AI. ~10 seconds. The first major cut, and the gate to everything that follows — so it’s worth seeing exactly how it’s built.

Each stock collapses to one number from 0 to 100. Every factor first becomes its own 0–100 sub-score, then blends with fixed weights that encode our view of a high-quality momentum setup:

FactorWeightWhat it rewards
Momentum (20-day return)25%Stocks that are actually trending up
Relative volume25%Conviction behind the move — volume confirms price
Breakout20%A clean technical event, not just drift
52-week proximity15%Strength — nearness to new highs
RSI15%Healthy momentum, not exhaustion

The detail that matters — each sub-score is shaped, not linear:

  • Momentum runs through a tanh curve (0% → 50, +25% → ~85, −25% → ~15) that saturates — so a stock up 200% on a one-off can’t dominate the board on a single number.
  • Relative volume is also tanh-shaped (1× → 50, 2× → ~75, 5× → 95+): rewarded, but capped so one anomalous day can’t run away with it.
  • Breakout is binary and decisive: fresh breakout = 100, breakdown = 20, neither = 50.
  • 52-week proximity is linear: at the high → 80, a new high → 100, down 50% → 0.
  • RSI uses a sweet spot, not “higher is better.” 50–70 scores best; oversold (<30) earns a bounce bonus; overbought (>70) decays — exhaustion is a risk, not a virtue. This is the line between a momentum screen and a momentum-quality screen.

The five blend, the market sorts, and the top 35 advance. Everything below is still scored and stored, but no further resources are spent on it.

Why 35? Tuned, not arbitrary. After the fact-check gate trims names whose theses can’t be fully verified (~30–35% on a strict bar), we still land on a displayed board of 20–25 fully-verified dossiers — comfortably above our floor of 15.

Stage 3 — Honest price levels (before any AI touches the name)

All 35 → a price cone each. Deterministic statistics. No AI. A 20,000-path Monte Carlo simulation over ~one month yields a downside (5th pct), midpoint, and upside (95th pct). The discipline: we zero out the drift. A naïve simulation bakes a recent rally into the forecast — momentum wearing a lab coat. Zeroing drift makes the cone describe volatility only; direction lives in the thesis. Pure math, so every name gets honest levels even when the AI budget is tight.

Three things make this hard to copy

Triangulation. A single dramatic signal is what fools retail screeners. We count one only when independent categories — price, volume, news, SEC/insider, options, crowd — confirm it; contradiction cuts the score 30%, and any source older than two days counts as absent. Several modest, confirming signals beat one spectacular but unconfirmed spike.

Smart money, four ways. Earnings-call tone vs. the prior four quarters, insider Form 4s (scheduled 10b5-1 sales down-weighted 5×), short-squeeze potential, and analyst-revision velocity — even Congressional trades. Notes that read as advice (“buy/sell/you must”) are rejected: this is research.

The critic runs on a different AI. The bull case is written by one model, the critique by another — no model marks its own homework — and its objection can pull a grade down, never up.

Stage 5 — The conviction grade (A / B / C / D)

Rules + quant models. No AI, no discretion. The grade starts from the composite score and earns bonuses only from corroborating models — Monte Carlo probability-up, Value-at-Risk outside the worst quartile, significant positive alpha (Fama-French), a DCF with sensible 20–50% upside (a DCF showing >50% earns nothing — treated as suspicious). Names grade on a curve — then hard caps bite, one direction only: a STRONG critic objection caps at B; a crisis volatility regime caps at C; poor simulation calibration or a cross-validation contradiction blocks an A. A grade can only move down. Nothing inflates one — a credibility guarantee.

The quality gates — why you never see a half-built pick

  1. Fact-check (mechanical, not AI judgment). Every number in the thesis must be tagged to the exact data point it came from; a deterministic checker confirms each claim has a tag, points to a key that exists, and matches within 2%. Invented or mis-cited numbers are caught automatically — the dossier is graded Verified, Partially Verified, or Unverified, and only the first two pass. This is our hallucination spine.
  2. Completeness. Catalyst + thesis + bull + bear + critic verdict + grade + price levels, with the bull and bear cases reading as finished — never truncated.
  3. Verification grade. The verdict must be Verified or Partially Verified.

Fail any gate and the name is hidden — a shorter clean board beats a padded one. If a pass leaves us short, the engine re-runs research only on the unfinished names until the board fills (it can only ever add complete dossiers), and a fresh scan replaces the dashboard only with at least 15 complete dossiers — otherwise the last good board stays up. Never empty, never thin, never broken.

What the client sees — and why it’s the moat

The dashboard shows the surviving board (typically 15–25 names), each with a complete dossier: catalyst, bull and bear, smart-money read, honest price levels, conviction grade. A separate page reports honest forward returns on past picks — winners and losers alike.

  2. Triangulation, not single-signal screening — with stale data treated as no data and contradictions penalised.
  3. A mechanical anti-hallucination spine — closed context, every number verified to its source; the biggest reason the research can be trusted.
